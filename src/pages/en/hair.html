The specialty

Locs, done patiently

A loc takes months to form and years to mature. Rushing any part of that — twisting too tight, retwisting too often, chasing a look the hair isn’t ready for — is what causes thinning, breakage at the root, and locs that never quite settle.

So the work here is deliberately slow. We look at your density, your texture, how your last set of roots grew in, and we build the appointment around that. If your hair needs rest rather than tension, you’ll be told so.

Whether you are starting your first set, coming from another loctician, or trying to rescue locs that have thinned or married together, there is a route forward.

Your appointment

What to expect

  1. Consultation

    We look at your hair and scalp together, talk about what you want, and agree on what is realistic today. If a service needs to be adjusted, you hear it before we start.

  2. Cleanse

    A clarifying or gentle wash depending on what your hair needs, followed by a scalp check. Locs are dried properly — never retwisted soaking wet.

  3. The work

    Sectioning, maintenance, repair or installation. This is the long part: bring headphones, a charger and something to drink.

  4. Finish & aftercare

    Styling, a final look in the mirror, and a plain-language explanation of how to care for your hair until the next visit — including when that visit should be.

Before you come in

How to prepare

A few small things make a long appointment go much better.

  • Come with clean, product-free hair unless your service includes a wash. No heavy oils, gels or butters on the roots.
  • Take down any previous style in advance — braids, twists or extensions should already be out.
  • Eat before you arrive and bring water. Longer installs can run several hours.
  • Arrive on time. More than 15 minutes late and the service may need to be shortened or rescheduled.
  • Come alone if you can. The studio is a small, calm space with room for one guest only when necessary.
  • Flag anything sensitive — scalp conditions, allergies, recent colour or chemical treatments, tenderness from a previous appointment.

Questions

Hair FAQ

How often should I get a retwist?
For most people, every four to eight weeks. Retwisting more often than that is one of the most common causes of thinning roots — the same section of hair simply cannot take that much tension. If your roots grow quickly, we can plan a rhythm that keeps them neat without over-working them.
Can I start locs on short hair?
Usually yes. Comb coils work on hair as short as about 5 cm (2 inches); two-strand twists generally need a little more length. We’ll confirm at the consultation and suggest the method that suits your texture and the loc size you’re aiming for.
Can I wash my locs myself?
Yes, and you should. Clean locs lock better than dirty ones. Use a clarifying, residue-free shampoo, focus on the scalp, rinse thoroughly, and dry them completely — trapped moisture is what causes odour and mildew. You’ll get a full routine after your first appointment.
My locs are thinning at the root. Can that be fixed?
Often, yes — if it is caught early. Depending on the cause we may reinforce the weak section, change the maintenance method, reduce how often the root is worked, or combine a thinning loc with a neighbour. Bring them in and we’ll assess honestly, including telling you if the answer is to cut and restart.
I’m coming from another loctician. Is that a problem?
Not at all — it happens often. Book a consultation or a maintenance appointment and mention it when you book, so enough time is set aside to look at the whole head before any work starts.
Do you work on children’s hair?
Yes, for braids, twists and loc maintenance. Please mention the child’s age when booking so the right amount of time is reserved, and plan for a shorter, gentler session.
