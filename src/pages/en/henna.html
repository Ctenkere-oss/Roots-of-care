The art

An old craft, one line at a time

Henna is a paste made from the dried, ground leaves of the henna plant. Applied to the skin, it leaves a stain that deepens over a day or two and fades gently over the following weeks. Nothing is injected, nothing is permanent.

Every design here is drawn by hand. No stencils, no transfers — which means your design can follow the shape of your hands, the occasion, and the amount of detail you actually want.

Bring a reference if you have one, or come with nothing at all and we’ll design as we go.

Good to know

How henna works

  1. Application

    The paste is piped onto clean, dry, oil-free skin. A small design takes 15 to 30 minutes; full bridal work can take several hours.

  2. Drying

    The paste sets in 20 to 30 minutes but keeps releasing colour for much longer. The longer you leave it on — ideally four to six hours, or overnight — the deeper the final stain.

  3. Removal

    Scrape or brush the dried paste off. Do not wash it off with water if you can help it, and keep the area away from water for the first 12 hours.

  4. Developing

    The stain starts bright orange and darkens to a deep reddish-brown over 24 to 48 hours. Its final shade depends on your skin, the part of the body, and your body heat — palms and soles always go darkest.

  5. Fading

    Expect roughly one to two weeks on hands and feet, less on areas you wash often or that rub against clothing. Henna fades, it does not peel off in one piece.

Make it last

Aftercare

Simple habits that add days to your design.

  • Leave the paste on as long as you can — four to six hours minimum, overnight if the design matters.
  • Keep warm. Heat deepens the stain. Cold hands stain lighter.
  • Avoid water for the first 12 hours after removing the paste, and skip swimming pools for the first day.
  • Seal with a natural oil — coconut, olive or shea — before showering, washing dishes or exercising.
  • Skip exfoliants. Scrubs, chlorine and harsh soaps strip the stain faster than anything else.
  • Pat dry, don’t rub. Friction is what removes the top layer of stained skin.

Questions

Henna FAQ

Do you use black henna?
Never — and neither should anyone else. So-called “black henna” is not henna. It contains para-phenylenediamine (PPD), a hair-dye chemical that is not approved for use on skin. It can cause chemical burns, permanent scarring and a lifelong allergy to hair dye and some medications. Natural henna is never black: it stains orange first, then reddish-brown. If someone offers you a design that goes black within an hour, walk away.
What is in your henna paste?
Henna powder, lemon juice, sugar and essential oils — that is all. The paste is mixed fresh, and we are happy to tell you exactly what is in the batch being used on you. If you have a known allergy, mention it when you book so a patch test can be done first.
Can I get henna if I’m pregnant?
Natural henna is widely used during pregnancy. Because everyone is different, mention it when you book — we can do a patch test and keep the essential oil content low. If you have any doubt, check with your healthcare provider first.
How far in advance should I book for a wedding?
For bridal work, as early as you can — two to three months is ideal for peak season. For the design itself, the sweet spot is one to two days before the event: the stain is at its darkest roughly 48 hours after application.
Will it stain my clothes or bedding?
Wet paste can transfer, so wear something you don’t mind marking and protect your sheets if you sleep with it on. Once the paste is removed, the stain is in your skin only and will not transfer to anything.
Can I choose my own design?
Absolutely. Bring photos, a theme, or a rough idea of how much of your hand or arm you want covered. Everything is drawn freehand, so the design can be adapted while we work.
