The art

An old craft, one line at a time

Both are plant dyes that stain the top layer of skin and fade with it. Nothing is injected, nothing is permanent, and neither contains harsh chemicals.

Every design here is drawn by hand. No stencils, no transfers — which means your design can follow the shape of your hands, the occasion, and the amount of detail you actually want.

Choosing

Henna or jagua?

Same freehand work, same application. The difference is the plant — and the colour it leaves behind.

Henna

Reddish-brown

Made from the dried, ground leaves of the Lawsonia inermis plant, used for thousands of years to create temporary skin art. 100% natural and free of harsh chemicals.

  • Stains bright orange first, deepening to reddish-brown over 24–48 hours
  • Darkest on hands and feet, lighter elsewhere
  • Roughly one to two weeks on hands
  • Contains no berry, eucalyptus or lavender ingredients

Jagua

Blue-black

A gel made from the fruit of the Genipa americana tree. It stains the skin temporarily, is 100% natural, and is free of harsh chemicals. It is the natural way to get a dark, tattoo-like line — not to be confused with “black henna”, which we never use.

  • Develops to a deep blue-black over about 48 hours
  • Reads much more like a real tattoo than henna does
  • Roughly one to two weeks, fading with the skin
  • Not suitable if you react to berries, eucalyptus or lavender

Before booking jagua

Do not book a jagua service if you have an allergy or sensitivity to berries, eucalyptus or lavender. Jagua comes from a fruit and the gel contains essential oils. If that applies to you, natural henna is the alternative — it contains none of those ingredients. A patch test is available on request for either.

Good to know

How it works

  1. Application

    The paste or gel is piped onto clean, dry, oil-free skin. A hand design takes about 15 minutes; an arm sleeve around 35.

  2. Drying

    It sets in 20 to 30 minutes but keeps releasing colour for much longer. The longer you leave it on — ideally four to six hours, or overnight — the deeper the final stain.

  3. Removal

    Scrape or brush the dried paste off. Don’t wash it off with water if you can help it, and keep the area away from water for the first 12 hours.

  4. Developing

    Henna starts bright orange and darkens to reddish-brown; jagua develops to a deep blue-black. Both take 24 to 48 hours to reach their final shade, which depends on your skin, the part of the body and your body heat.

  5. Fading

    Expect roughly one to two weeks on hands, less on areas you wash often or that rub against clothing. It fades gradually — it does not peel off in one piece.

Make it last

Aftercare

Simple habits that add days to your design.

  • Leave the paste on as long as you can — four to six hours minimum, overnight if the design matters.
  • Keep warm. Heat deepens the stain. Cold hands stain lighter.
  • Avoid water for the first 12 hours after removing the paste, and skip swimming pools for the first day.
  • Seal with a natural oil — coconut, olive or shea — before showering, washing dishes or exercising.
  • Skip exfoliants. Scrubs, chlorine and harsh soaps strip the stain faster than anything else.
  • Pat dry, don’t rub. Friction is what removes the top layer of stained skin.

Questions

Body art FAQ

Do you use black henna?
Never — and neither should anyone else. So-called “black henna” is not henna. It contains para-phenylenediamine (PPD), a hair-dye chemical that is not approved for use on skin. It can cause chemical burns, permanent scarring and a lifelong allergy to hair dye and some medications. Natural henna is never black: it stains orange first, then reddish-brown. If you want a dark, tattoo-like design, jagua is the safe, natural way to get it — that is exactly what it is for.
Who shouldn’t book jagua?
Anyone with an allergy or sensitivity to berries, eucalyptus or lavender. Jagua gel is made from the fruit of the Genipa americana tree and contains essential oils, so a berry allergy is a real reason to choose henna instead. If you’re unsure, ask for a patch test when you book.
What is in your henna paste?
Henna powder, lemon juice, sugar and essential oils — that is all. It is mixed fresh, and we are happy to tell you exactly what is in the batch being used on you. Henna is a natural, reddish-brown dye derived from the leaves of the Lawsonia inermis plant, used for thousands of years to create temporary skin art.
Why do prices sometimes change?
The listed price covers a design of typical density for that area. A much more intricate piece takes longer, so the price can go up — but you will always be told before any work starts, never after.
Can I get body art if I’m pregnant?
Natural henna is widely used during pregnancy. Because everyone is different, mention it when you book — we can do a patch test and keep the essential oil content low. If you have any doubt, check with your healthcare provider first.
How far in advance should I book for a wedding?
For bridal work, as early as you can — two to three months is ideal for peak season. For the design itself, the sweet spot is one to two days before the event: the stain is at its darkest roughly 48 hours after application.
Will it stain my clothes or bedding?
Wet paste can transfer, so wear something you don’t mind marking and protect your sheets if you sleep with it on. Once the paste is removed, the stain is in your skin only and will not transfer to anything.
Can I choose my own design?
Absolutely. Bring photos, a theme, or a rough idea of how much of your hand or arm you want covered. Everything is drawn freehand, so the design can be adapted while we work.
